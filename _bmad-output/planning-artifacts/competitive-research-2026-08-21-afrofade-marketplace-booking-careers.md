# Afrofade — Competitive Research

## 0. Executive conclusion

The market is **not empty**. Booking, salon SaaS, local beauty discovery, independent professional listings and even beauty-sector recruitment already exist in Africa.

Therefore Afrofade should **not** position itself as merely:

> "the app where you can find and book a hair salon"

or:

> "LinkedIn for barbers/hairdressers"

Those ideas already have direct or adjacent implementations.

The strongest defendable Afrofade thesis is the integrated **hair-only decision graph**:

```text
DISCOVER A LOOK
    -> TRY / CUSTOMIZE IT ON YOUR OWN HEAD
    -> SAVE THE EXACT LOOK
    -> MATCH WITH NEARBY PROFESSIONALS WHO CAN EXECUTE THAT STYLE
    -> BOOK THE RIGHT PROFESSIONAL / SALON
    -> SEND THE SAVED LOOK INTO THE APPOINTMENT
    -> COMPLETE SERVICE
    -> VERIFIED REVIEW + PORTFOLIO / SKILL SIGNAL
```

In parallel, the same skill taxonomy can support careers:

```text
Professional skills + verified portfolio + completed services
    -> professional reputation
    -> job matching
    -> salon recruiting
```

Afrofade's moat is therefore not any single feature. It is the **connection between 3D visual intent, structured hair skills, local supply, booking and professional reputation**.

# 1. Burkina Faso — current observed landscape

## 1.1 No clearly dominant dedicated hair marketplace was found

Research found individual salons and generic/local marketplaces with online presence, but no clearly dominant Burkina-first platform combining salon discovery, professional identities, live booking, recruitment and AI/3D hair try-on.

This is an opportunity, but it must not be interpreted as proof that there is no competition. The strongest competition is likely fragmented across:

- WhatsApp/phone booking;
- Instagram/Facebook/TikTok discovery;
- Google Maps/business pages;
- individual salon websites;
- generic marketplace/service directories;
- informal recruitment posts/groups.

### Observed Burkina examples

**Beauty Réservation BF**
- Ouagadougou, Secteur 17;
- online appointment scheduling via a generic booking infrastructure;
- demonstrates local willingness to expose beauty appointments online;
- does not represent an observed full marketplace/network product.

Source: https://booking.page/pt/company/page/beautyrdvpro

**Das'hair**
- Burkina hair-care/salon brand;
- accepts online appointment requests and then confirms with the client;
- shows that established salons can digitize booking directly without a marketplace intermediary.

Source: https://dashair.bf/faq

**Ouagadougou.online / Bella Dona listing**
- beauty services can already be sold/booked through generic local commerce directories;
- demonstrates that generic marketplaces can compete for the transaction even without hair-specific software.

Source: https://www.ouagadougou.online/produit/prestations-de-salon-de-coiffure-beaute/

**Fasoloogo**
- Burkina generic marketplace for products and services;
- positioning includes helping local service providers find nearby customers;
- not hair-specialized but an adjacent acquisition channel.

Source: https://fasoloogo.biz/

### Burkina implication

Afrofade's early competition in Burkina will likely be less "another identical startup" and more **existing user habits**:

```text
Customer discovery     -> Instagram / TikTok / Facebook / word of mouth
Contact                 -> WhatsApp / call
Availability            -> manual conversation
Booking                 -> informal confirmation
Style reference         -> screenshot/photo
Recruitment             -> WhatsApp/Facebook/general job posts
Professional reputation -> personal social accounts / word of mouth
```

Afrofade must beat this workflow on convenience and trust without demanding excessive complexity.

# 2. West Africa / Francophone Africa — strongest observed competitors

## 2.1 Planify — Côte d'Ivoire

Source: https://planify-ci.com/

Observed positioning:
- beauty marketplace in Côte d'Ivoire;
- search by service, city and geolocation;
- customer can choose service, professional and available slot;
- public salon pages with photos, services and reviews;
- salon CRM, revenue analytics and WhatsApp/SMS/email marketing;
- multi-user / multi-salon Business plan;
- payments can use CinetPay, Orange Money, MTN Mobile Money and Wave according to CGU.

Observed pricing (2026):
- Free: up to 30 bookings/month;
- Pro: 15,000 FCFA/month;
- Business: 35,000 FCFA/month, including multiple salons.

Competitive strength vs Afrofade:
- strong booking/operations baseline;
- local/mobile-money context;
- geolocation and marketplace are already part of the product.

Observed gap relative to proposed Afrofade:
- no demonstrated manipulable 3D hairstyle try-on flow;
- no demonstrated hair-skill professional career graph/recruitment layer;
- broader beauty scope rather than hair-only depth.

---

## 2.2 Lilix — Côte d'Ivoire / Senegal

Source: https://www.lilix.store/

Observed positioning:
- mobile-first salon SaaS;
- shareable booking link optimized for WhatsApp;
- clients do not need to install an app;
- deposits via Wave / Orange Money;
- automated reminders;
- independent professional plan;
- team plan;
- multi-salon plan.

Observed 2026 claims/evidence:
- site claims 150+ salons in Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal;
- public salon directory showed live listings in Abidjan and Dakar at research time.

Observed pricing:
- Starter: 4,900 FCFA/month, aimed at independent providers / small teams, plus 3% online-payment commission;
- Pro: 9,900 FCFA/month, unlimited employees, 0% platform commission on direct payments;
- Multi-salon: 19,900 FCFA/month, up to 5 sites.

Competitive lesson for Afrofade:
- African salons value **phone-first onboarding, WhatsApp distribution and Mobile Money**, not desktop-heavy SaaS;
- Afrofade's future independent-professional and multi-salon pricing must compete with concrete regional price anchors.

Observed gap relative to proposed Afrofade:
- primarily salon operating/booking infrastructure;
- no demonstrated 3D visual decision engine;
- no observed dedicated professional recruitment marketplace.

## 2.5 Tokoss — Francophone African cities

Source: https://www.tokoss.app/

Observed positioning:
- beauty marketplace;
- portfolios and reviews;
- booking;
- nearby providers;
- current stated cities: Abidjan, Kinshasa, Dakar, Douala, Libreville;
- AI skin and hair analysis: skin tone, undertone, hydration, hair type 4A–4C, porosity, density;
- professional recommendations based on user needs;
- web available; mobile app described as coming soon.

Important evidence caution:
- the same landing page showed placeholder-like counters near the top (`0+`) while later claiming 500+ verified providers in five cities. Traction claims should therefore be independently verified before using them in market sizing.

Competitive strength:
- this is a meaningful competitor to an "AI + discovery + booking" story.

Observed differentiation opportunity for Afrofade:
- Tokoss AI is presented primarily as **diagnostic/personalization**, not a demonstrated canonical 3D head + manipulable hairstyle asset try-on system;
- Tokoss is broad beauty (nails, makeup, spa, skin, hair), while Afrofade can go much deeper on hair/barber execution.

## 2.6 OUISALON — Francophone Africa

Source: https://ouisalon.com/

Observed positioning:
- booking marketplace across Francophone Africa;
- categories include hair, barber, nails, institutes, massage, etc.;
- its directory explicitly targets Dakar, Abidjan, Douala, Yaoundé, Bamako, **Ouagadougou**, Lomé, Cotonou, Niamey and Libreville.

Evidence caution:
- the homepage contained unresolved `Chargement...` sections during research;
- very large impact metrics are displayed, but this research did not independently verify those figures;
- listing a city in an SEO directory does not by itself prove deep active supply in that city.

Competitive implication:
- Afrofade should assume a pan-francophone booking player can enter Burkina quickly even if local liquidity is initially weak.

Observed gap vs proposed Afrofade:
- no demonstrated 3D hairstyle decision engine;
- no observed hair-specific professional reputation/career graph.

# 3. Broader African competitors / adjacent products

## 3.1 GlamAfric — Nigeria — closest functional threat

Sources:
- https://www.linkedin.com/company/glamafric
- 2026 software listings (Capterra/GetApp)

Observed/currently described capabilities:
- African beauty SaaS + marketplace;
- customers discover and book professionals;
- appointment scheduling;
- staff/client management;
- online payments;
- independent professionals and salons;
- multi-location business support;
- marketing/product sales;
- **hiring / recruitment** appears in company and software descriptions.

This is the most important competitive finding.

Afrofade cannot claim that combining booking + salon management + recruitment is unique.

Historical AI signal:
- in 2023, GlamAfric's founder publicly discussed AI recommendations, image recognition and virtual try-on as possible V2 directions.
- this research did not find current evidence that GlamAfric operates a comparable real manipulable 3D head/hair asset pipeline in production.

Competitive consequence:
- Afrofade must differentiate at the **hair decision + execution data layer**, not at the generic feature checklist level.

# 4. International concept analogs that validate the direction

These are not Burkina/African direct competitors but are strategically important because they show that the integrated concept is emerging globally.

## 4.1 HairIQ

Source: https://hairsiq.com/

Observed positioning:
- Afro-hair specialist marketplace;
- nearby specialists;
- portfolios, reviews and booking;
- AI hair diagnostics;
- matching score based on hair profile and stylist specialties;
- Professional and Salon subscriptions plus booking fee.

Observed pricing at research time:
- Professional: US$24.99/month;
- Salon: US$59.99/month;
- 10% platform fee per completed booking.

Important maturity caution:
- page also contains placeholder/zero counters and states mobile apps are coming soon; treat traction as unverified.

Strategic lesson:
- **AI profile -> skill matching -> booking** is a credible concept and therefore should not be treated as inherently unique.
- Afrofade's stronger opportunity is to use a **visual style/3D asset** as part of the matching and booking contract.

---

## 4.2 Salonect — India

Source: https://www.salonect.com/

This is the closest observed conceptual analogue to the expanded Afrofade vision.

Observed features:
- nearby salon discovery;
- booking and advance payment;
- AI hair scan;
- AI styling preview;
- saved style/book flow;
- salon management;
- **salon hiring / stylist job discovery**;
- product marketplace.

Its positioning literally connects preview to booking.

Why it matters:
- it validates that the combination `visual decision + booking + salon operations + hiring` is not irrational;
- it also proves that Afrofade cannot rely on the broad idea alone as a global moat.

Afrofade differentiation must therefore come from:
- Africa/Francophone/mobile-money execution;
- Afro/textured hair specialization;
- deeper 3D manipulation rather than only selfie/image preview;
- canonical hair asset catalog;
- structured execution skills;
- exact look attached to booking;
- professional reputation and recruitment based on real hair outcomes.

# 6. Where Afrofade truly adds value

## 6.1 Weak differentiators — do not lead with these

These are valuable features but already common/observable:

- find salons nearby;
- choose a professional;
- book an appointment;
- receive reminders;
- online payment/deposit;
- professional portfolio;
- ratings/reviews;
- salon CRM;
- multi-salon dashboard;
- independent professional accounts;
- even recruitment/hiring.

Afrofade needs them because users expect them, not because they are the moat.

## 6.2 Stronger differentiated layer

### A. Visual intent becomes structured data

Instead of a customer saying:

> "Je veux quelque chose comme ça"

and sending an arbitrary screenshot, Afrofade can create a durable `SavedLook/TryOnAsset` containing:

- exact hairstyle asset/version;
- head/fit configuration;
- color;
- line-up/detail parameters;
- beard options later;
- visual preview;
- user notes.

That object can travel with the booking.

### B. Match by the style the user actually chose

Afrofade can rank providers based on:

```text
user location
+ selected hairstyle taxonomy
+ professional verified specialties
+ portfolio evidence
+ rating
+ availability
+ price
= execution match
```

This is stronger than generic `salon near me`.

### C. Hair-only depth

Broad beauty competitors spread product scope across hair, nails, spa, massage, makeup, skincare, etc.

Afrofade can specialize in:

- fades;
- braids/tresses;
- locks/locs;
- twists;
- waves;
- natural/textured hair;
- color;
- line-up;
- beard/facial hair.

This supports a much richer skill graph and style catalog.

### D. 3D rather than only AI selfie generation

Afrofade's planned architecture uses separable canonical assets:

```text
CanonicalHead
+ CanonicalHairAsset
-> HairFitter
-> interactive TryOn
```

That enables repeated style swaps, controlled color/details, consistent viewing angles and later beard/accessory modules without regenerating a flat image for every action.

### E. Reputation can become outcome-linked

A verified completed booking can link:

```text
requested style
-> responsible professional
-> completed appointment
-> final portfolio/result
-> client review
```

Over time Afrofade could know not just that "Ibrahim is a barber", but that he has repeatedly delivered high-rated low taper fades, waves, beard fades, loc retwists, etc.

That data can power both booking and recruitment.

# 7. Most dangerous competitors

## Threat 1 — Existing habits: WhatsApp + social media

Highest short-term threat in Burkina.

Advantages:
- already installed;
- free;
- everyone understands it;
- salons already have audiences there.

Afrofade response:
- do not force app download for every customer action;
- support shareable web booking/profile/look URLs;
- use WhatsApp as a distribution channel, not an enemy;
- make the value after clicking the link obvious.

## Threat 2 — Regional booking SaaS (Lilix / Planify / SunuSalon)

They can solve 60–80% of ordinary salon booking/operations needs without expensive 3D technology.

Afrofade response:
- do not over-invest in commodity calendar/CRM complexity at the expense of visual decision + matching;
- compete on customer acquisition and style conversion, not only back-office tooling.

## Threat 3 — GlamAfric

Closest African feature-set competitor because it already describes marketplace + salon SaaS + independent professionals + multi-location + hiring.

Afrofade response:
- position around **hair execution intelligence**, not generic beauty management.

## Threat 4 — Future entrants copying AI preview

Generative hairstyle previews are becoming easy to build.

Afrofade response:
- the moat cannot be "we call an image model";
- own the canonical 3D catalog, skill taxonomy, fitting data, salon/professional supply, booking history and outcome/reputation graph.

# 8. Product strategy recommendation

## Recommended positioning

> **Afrofade helps you decide exactly what hair look you want, find the nearby professional who can actually execute it, and book them — while giving hair professionals and salons the tools to grow their reputation, clientele and team.**

Short form:

> **Try the look. Find the skill. Book the chair.**

French product expression candidate:

> **Essaie. Trouve le bon pro. Réserve.**

## What Afrofade should NOT become

- a generic beauty super-app;
- a clone of Planify/Lilix with a 3D button;
- a generic job board;
- a social network with feed/followers before marketplace liquidity exists;
- a full ERP/POS before the core booking loop works.

## Strategic product loop

```text
More styles / better try-on
        ↓
More consumer intent
        ↓
More bookings
        ↓
More verified professional outcomes
        ↓
Better professional ranking & portfolios
        ↓
Better marketplace trust
        ↓
More salons/professionals join
        ↓
More availability & geographic coverage
        ↓
More consumer value
```

Recruitment then becomes a derivative advantage:

```text
verified skills + real portfolio + marketplace reputation
        ↓
better hiring signals than a generic CV
```

# 9. Implications for BMAD Correct Course

Before implementation, the future Change Proposal should preserve these rules:

1. Booking/local discovery are **expected capabilities**, not the primary moat.
2. Professional identity must remain separate from salon entities.
3. Multi-salon ownership must be first-class.
4. Independent professional commercial use is paid.
5. Hair professionals affiliated with paying salons receive salon-scoped capabilities without mandatory duplicate subscription.
6. A common `HairStyle/SkillTaxonomy` should connect:
   - 3D catalog assets;
   - salon services;
   - professional specialties;
   - portfolios;
   - bookings;
   - job requirements.
7. `SavedLook/TryOnAsset` should be attachable to a booking.
8. Reviews should eventually be tied to completed bookings to become verified reputation signals.
9. Marketplace V1 should be mobile-first and WhatsApp-friendly.
10. Recruitment should use the same professional identity/reputation system instead of creating a disconnected CV database.

# 10. Research limitations

- Public website claims are not equivalent to audited traction.
- Several startup landing pages contain placeholder counters or claims that could not be independently verified.
- A feature marked `NO OBSERVED` means this research did not find a public demonstration of it; it is not proof that the company has never built or planned it.
- Burkina's informal workflows are under-indexed on searchable websites because much activity happens through WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, calls and walk-ins.
- A later field study should interview salons, independent professionals and consumers in Ouagadougou/Bobo-Dioulasso to validate willingness to pay and booking behavior.
