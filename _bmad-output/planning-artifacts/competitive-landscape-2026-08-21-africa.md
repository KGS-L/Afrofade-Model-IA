# Competitive Landscape — 2026-08-21

This artifact is a time-stamped market-research snapshot. Product implementation must not depend on competitors remaining unchanged.

## 1. Executive conclusion

Afrofade is entering a market where **booking and salon-management software already exists in francophone Africa**. Therefore, booking alone is not defensible.

The strongest observed opportunity is to combine capabilities that current visible competitors do not demonstrate as one integrated hair-specific loop:

```text
3D visual intent
 -> canonical hairstyle/style identity
 -> nearby skill-aware discovery
 -> salon / independent professional
 -> real availability
 -> booking with saved visual brief
 -> completed service
 -> verified review / professional reputation
 -> careers / hiring signal
```

Afrofade should win on this connected hair-specific data model and workflow, not on having the longest generic feature checklist.

## 3. GlamAfric — important historical competitor and cautionary case

Historical sources describe GlamAfric as a beauty marketplace + SaaS platform supporting:

- consumer discovery;
- professional portfolios;
- booking/payment;
- salon operations;
- multi-location support;
- hiring/job board;
- style inspiration.

This is extremely close to the non-3D parts of Afrofade's expanded vision and proves that **booking + marketplace + careers by itself is not a novel category**.

However, current research also surfaces a founder/family blog archive titled around a bootstrapped SaaS startup that failed, including entries explicitly named:

- “Sunsetting the job boards”;
- “Sunsetting GlamAfric Appointment Booking Platform.”

Source snapshot: https://www.thefblog.co.uk/archive

Historical public recruitment activity also clusters around 2021-2022/early 2023 in retrieved sources. The original operational website is not relied upon as a currently active competitor in this research snapshot.

### Lesson for Afrofade

Do not copy the failed super-platform breadth blindly. GlamAfric is evidence that having marketplace + booking + jobs + back office does **not automatically solve adoption**.

Afrofade needs a sharper wedge:

1. hair + beard only;
2. visual decision engine (3D try-on);
3. style-aware professional matching;
4. booking that carries the exact requested look;
5. verified service/reputation loop;
6. Africa/francophone operational fit;
7. phased rollout instead of launching every possible vertical feature simultaneously.

## 5. Afrofade defensible value proposition

The product should not market itself as “another salon booking app.”

Recommended positioning:

> **Afrofade helps people decide exactly how they want to look, find a nearby hair professional who can execute that style, and book with the visual reference already attached — while giving hair professionals and salons a trusted identity, customer-acquisition channel and hiring network.**

### Differentiation stack

**Layer 1 — Visual intent moat**

Consumer creates/owns a 3D head and tries reusable hairstyle assets. Color, beard and line-up extensions enrich the requested look.

**Layer 2 — Hair taxonomy moat**

The same canonical taxonomy links 3D assets, skills, services, portfolio work, bookings and jobs.

**Layer 3 — Conversion loop**

A saved look does not end at “download”; it leads to nearby qualified availability and booking.

**Layer 4 — Reputation data**

Completed bookings create verified salon/professional reputation around actual services/styles.

**Layer 5 — Vertical careers**

The professional identity used for customer acquisition is also the identity used to apply for hair-industry jobs.

## 6. Strategic warnings

1. **Do not launch as a feature clone of GlamAfric.** Historical breadth did not guarantee survival.
2. **Do not make online payment a launch blocker.** Booking can validate demand with pay-at-provider first.
3. **Do not build a social feed before liquidity.** Discovery, booking and careers must solve concrete transactions first.
4. **Do not force every professional into a salon.** Independent providers are common and commercially relevant.
5. **Do not hard-code pricing into identity/data model.** Competitor pricing varies substantially.
6. **Do not rank purely by sponsorship.** Organic relevance must preserve trust.
7. **Do not weaken the 3D core.** It is the clearest differentiation from the visible regional booking products.

## 7. Market-validation metrics to instrument from day one

- consumer search -> profile view rate;
- profile view -> booking-start rate;
- booking-start -> confirmed booking rate;
- % bookings carrying hairstyle/style ID;
- % bookings carrying saved try-on visual brief;
- median distance to booked provider;
- availability hit rate;
- salon acquisition source attributed to Afrofade;
- independent professional subscription conversion;
- salon subscription conversion;
- repeat booking rate;
- completed booking -> verified review rate;
- job post -> qualified application rate;
- application -> interview/hire rate;
- 3D try-on -> marketplace search conversion;
- 3D try-on -> booking conversion.

These metrics are more useful for product-market fit than raw sign-up counts.
